## Human

Watched a video where an AI built an animated horse in Three.js on a skeleton,
and it looked far better than our character generator. Asked why; after the
explanation (our rig is a one-shot SDF construction aid, the horse skins a mesh
and animates bones), asked to prototype a skinned preview on one figure.
Constraint: ignore most past conventions, but the output must stay printable.

## Assistant

## Key decisions

**Mesh once, skin, animate in rig-parameter space.** The default Character
Creator figure is built once in an A-pose (arms clear of the torso) and skinned
to 15 bones. Animations are written as rig pose params, so each frame is also a
valid `F.rig({pose})`, and `print-pose.mjs` rebuilds any frame as a watertight,
one-piece SDF solid. Printability comes from the SDF rebuild, never from the
skinned mesh.

**Bone transforms from the real rig, not re-implemented FK.** `F.rig()` costs
about 0.06 ms, so the viewer calls it every frame and maps each bone's rest
frame onto its posed frame. Frames come from the rig's own outputs (limb dirs,
elbow/knee hinges, hand splay, head axes, waist ring), so preview and print
can't drift apart and limb twist survives.

**Weights: radius-normalized distance + midline mask + edge diffusion.** Raw
distance-to-bone let the thin upper-arm bone claim torso flanks; dividing by
each bone's radius fixes that. Diffusion over mesh edges softens joints. This
is cheaper than bone-heat and good enough for a prototype.

**Kept out of the app.** It lives in `prototypes/`, served by the dev server,
and imports `sdfFigure.ts` directly. The baked mesh is gitignored (10 MB) and
regenerated by `export.mjs`.
